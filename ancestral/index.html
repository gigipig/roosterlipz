🧬 How We Calculate Your Results

Overview

Your results are calculated using two well-established principles from population genetics: Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium and Mendelian inheritance. These methods let us estimate your likely genetic traits based on your grandparents' ancestral populations.

Hardy-Weinberg Equilibrium

When we know how common a genetic variant is in a population (e.g., "89% of Nordic people carry the lactase persistence allele"), Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium helps us calculate the probability of different genotypes (two copies, one copy, or no copies of the variant).

If p = frequency of variant allele, and q = 1 - p:

  • p² = probability of two copies (homozygous)
  • 2pq = probability of one copy (heterozygous)
  • q² = probability of no copies

This assumes random mating within a population, which is a reasonable approximation for estimating genotype frequencies from allele frequencies.

Mendelian Inheritance

Once we have genotype probabilities for your grandparents (from their populations), we simulate inheritance using Mendelian genetics — the same principles Gregor Mendel discovered with pea plants.

1
Grandparents → Parents

Each parent inherits one allele from each of their parents (your grandparents).

2
Parents → You

You inherit one allele from your mother and one from your father.

3
Probability Calculation

We calculate the probability of each possible genotype you could have inherited.

Inheritance Patterns

Different traits follow different inheritance patterns:

Dominant

One copy is enough to express the trait (e.g., lactase persistence). If either parent passes the variant, you likely have the trait.

Recessive

Two copies needed to express the trait (e.g., some Arctic adaptations). Both parents must pass the variant.

Additive

Effect scales with copies (e.g., AMY1 starch digestion). More copies = stronger effect. We average across your ancestry.

Important Limitations

  • Population-level data: We use average allele frequencies for populations, not your actual DNA.
  • Probabilistic results: Results show likelihood, not certainty. Your actual genetics may differ.
  • Simplified model: Real inheritance involves many genes, environmental factors, and gene interactions we don't model.
  • Historical populations: Our data represents traditional/historical populations, which may differ from modern populations.

Data Sources

Our genetic data comes from peer-reviewed studies on population genetics, including research on lactase persistence, AMY1 copy number variation, FADS fatty acid metabolism, and population-specific adaptations. Dietary information is based on anthropological and archaeological research.

📖 About Ancestral Diet Explorer

What is this?

Ancestral Diet Explorer helps you discover dietary patterns that may align with your genetic heritage. By entering your grandparents' ancestral backgrounds, we estimate genetic traits related to food metabolism and suggest traditional foods from your lineage.

Why Grandparents?

We use grandparent ancestry because it captures genetic diversity while remaining simple. Your four grandparents represent four distinct genetic lineages, allowing us to model Mendelian inheritance patterns more accurately than a single "ethnicity percentage."

What You Get

  • Genetic trait predictions: Likelihood of traits like lactose tolerance, starch digestion efficiency, and caffeine metabolism
  • Traditional foods: Foods historically consumed by your ancestral populations
  • Dietary insights: Personalized recommendations based on your predicted genetic profile
  • Educational content: Learn about the science behind nutrigenomics

Important Note

This tool is for educational purposes only. Results are probabilistic estimates based on population-level data, not actual DNA testing. For medical or dietary advice, consult a healthcare professional or registered dietitian.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Is this the same as a DNA test?

No. DNA tests like 23andMe analyze your actual genetic material. We use population-level statistics to estimate what traits you might have based on where your grandparents came from. Think of it as "genetic probability" rather than "genetic certainty."

How accurate are the results?

Results reflect population averages and Mendelian inheritance probabilities. If we say "75% likely lactose tolerant," it means 3 out of 4 people with your ancestral mix would have this trait. Your individual genetics may differ.

Why can't I find my specific ethnicity?

We group populations by genetic similarity and available research data. If your specific group isn't listed, try selecting a geographically or culturally related population. We're continuously adding more populations as research becomes available.

Should I change my diet based on these results?

This tool is for exploration and education, not medical advice. If you're considering dietary changes, especially for health conditions, consult a registered dietitian or healthcare provider who can consider your complete health picture.

Is my data saved or shared?

All calculations happen in your browser. Your ancestry selections are saved locally on your device (localStorage) so you don't have to re-enter them. We don't collect, store, or share any personal information.

Can I use this if I'm adopted or don't know my ancestry?

You can explore different ancestries to learn about various dietary traditions and genetic adaptations. The "DNA Test" mode also lets you enter percentages if you have results from an ancestry service.

🔒 Privacy

Your Privacy Matters

Ancestral Diet Explorer is designed with privacy as a core principle. We believe your genetic and ancestral information is deeply personal and should stay that way.

What We Don't Do

  • No data collection: We don't collect, store, or transmit your ancestry selections to any server
  • No tracking: We don't use analytics, cookies, or tracking pixels
  • No accounts: No sign-up required, no email collection
  • No third parties: Your data is never shared with advertisers or data brokers

How It Works

Everything runs locally in your web browser. When you select ancestries and calculate results, the computation happens on your device using JavaScript. The only data stored is in your browser's localStorage, which you can clear at any time through your browser settings.
