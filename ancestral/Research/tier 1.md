# Population-specific allele frequencies for Tier 1 nutrigenomics variants

The six nutrigenomics variants studied show **striking population-specific patterns** that have major implications for precision nutrition interventions. Most notably, MTHFR 677T reaches **66%** in Mexican Indigenous populations but only **1-5%** in Southern Africans, while HFE C282Y ranges from **11%** in Ireland to **0%** in most non-European populations. These dramatic differences mean nutrigenomic recommendations cannot be generalized across ancestries.

This analysis synthesizes data from gnomAD v4, 1000 Genomes Phase 3, HGDP, ALFA, and peer-reviewed studies across 13 global population groups. **Data gaps remain significant** for Oceanian, Central Asian, and specific African sub-populations in all major databases.

## HFE rs1800562 confirms strict European specificity

The hemochromatosis **A allele** (C282Y) is essentially a European-specific variant with a **Celtic/Nordic peak**. Ireland shows the global maximum at **10–11%**, declining to **2–3%** in Mediterranean Europe and **<0.5%** in all non-European populations. This is the clearest example of a clinically-actionable variant with sharply ancestry-restricted relevance.

## Data gaps and limitations affect clinical translation

Several population groups have insufficient representation in genomic databases, limiting precision nutrition implementation:

**Severe data gaps (most variants unavailable):**
- Aboriginal Australian
- Melanesian / Papua New Guinean  
- Khoisan / San populations
- Specific North African (Maghreb, Egyptian)
- Central Asian (Kazakh, Uzbek, Mongolian)
- Amazonian Indigenous

**Moderate gaps (some variants missing):**
- Ethiopian / Somali
- Southeast Asian subpopulations (Thai, Filipino, Indonesian)
- Pacific Islander (Polynesian, Micronesian)

## Clinical interpretation varies dramatically by ancestry

The nutrigenomic impact of these variants differs substantially across populations:

**Highest clinical relevance by population:**

| Variant | High-Priority Populations | Low-Priority Populations |
|---------|--------------------------|-------------------------|
| CYP1A2 C (caffeine) | West/East African, South Asian (43–48%) | European North, Oceanian (22–33%) |
| APOA2 C (sat. fat) | European, Middle Eastern (32–45%) | East Asian (6–9%) |
| MTHFR T (folate) | Indigenous American, Southern European (42–66%) | Sub-Saharan African (1–11%) |
| HFE A (iron) | Northern European only (3–11%) | All non-Europeans (<0.5%) |
| TCF7L2 T (diabetes) | Middle Eastern (39–41%) | East Asian (2–5%) |
| FTO A (obesity) | West African, Middle Eastern (48–53%) | Indigenous American, East Asian (1–20%) |

## Conclusion

These six nutrigenomics variants demonstrate that **population ancestry is the dominant factor** in determining allele frequency distributions. HFE C282Y represents an extreme case of European specificity, while MTHFR C677T shows a **66-fold range** between Mexican Indigenous and Southern African populations. 

For clinical nutrigenomics implementation, **ancestry-specific reference frequencies are essential**—using global averages would misclassify risk for most populations. The persistent data gaps for Oceanian, Central Asian, and many African subpopulations represent a critical barrier to equitable precision nutrition. Future population-specific sequencing initiatives should prioritize these underrepresented groups to enable evidence-based nutrigenomic recommendations across all ancestries.
